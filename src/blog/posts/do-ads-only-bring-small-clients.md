"Big clients don't come from Facebook ads."

I hear this from a lot of B2B firms. And honestly, there's some truth in it.

A property finance broker told me their biggest deals come "through my network," and that those buyers mostly have "trusted partners" already. An executive search firm owner said the big companies "just call their friends on their boards."

The data backs them up a little. In the [2026 Kitces study of financial advisors](https://www.kitces.com/kitces-report-financial-planner-advisor-marketing-tactics-strategies-referrals-centers-influence-networking-2026/), clients who came in through advertising brought in about $5,000 a year, versus about $11,000 for the average new client.

So yes. The first deal from an ad is often smaller.

BUT!

## Big clients rarely start big anyway

A training consultant told me how their best client relationships actually begin. It's very rare that a company commits to a full program up front.

They buy one pilot day.

If it goes well, they book more. One of their biggest clients, a construction company with thousands of employees, started with a head of training who said they didn't use outside trainers at all. They agreed to a pilot. Now that consultant is one of the only outside trainers the company uses, eight or nine days a year.

That client came in through a referral. But look at the shape of it. Small first step, then growth.

That's how most B2B relationships work, whether the first contact is a referral, a conference or an ad. Nobody hands a stranger a six-figure contract on day one.

So the question isn't "will ads bring me big clients?" It's "will ads bring me good first steps with companies that could become big clients?"

## Advertise the first step, not the whole thing

This is where a lot of firms go wrong. They run ads for the big engagement.

A stranger sees "12-month leadership transformation program" and thinks: I don't know you. I'm not committing to a year.

Instead, advertise the thing a stranger can actually say yes to. A diagnostic. A pilot. A short paid project that solves one problem.

The ad gets them in the door. Your work does what it always does after that.

## Advertise for the clients you want more of

The other mistake is advertising whatever you happen to sell.

That property finance broker does a lot of small, steady deals that "keep the lights on," and wants more of the big commercial ones. So when I walked them through how we'd run it, the plan was simple. We only advertise the big deals. The small ones keep coming in the way they always have.

What the ad talks about decides who responds to it. An ad about a £500 job attracts people with £500 problems.

I run an agency that runs ads on Facebook, Instagram and Google for B2B firms and books calls with buyers on their calendars.

Before real money goes in, we test a handful of short ads, each describing a different problem your best clients had, and see which ones the right companies respond to. When someone taps an ad, they answer a few questions about their company before they can book a call with you. Company size is one of them. Too small, and they never get offered a time.
